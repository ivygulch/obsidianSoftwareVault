The parenthetical in this posting's title is the part I want to do: iOS with technical leadership responsibility. I spent the last five years in that seat. The engineering standards I defined for architecture, performance, security, and testing were built against by an iOS organization of 120+ engineers across the Walmart, ASDA, and Sam's Club apps. When I argued for technical investment, those were the numbers I argued with.

I worked with Walmart's backend teams on the GraphQL schema and mobile-optimized APIs for checkout and payments, collaborated regularly with the search and discovery teams through the inner-sourcing model on features that spanned multiple app modules, and led multi-team delivery and release readiness on a large inner-sourced codebase with frequent production releases. The architectural patterns I established on the small initial team during the 2020 ground-up rewrite of the Walmart app became the foundation I then owned and evolved as Principal from 2021 to 2026, with each year's feature delivery laddering into the modularization and maintainability direction. At CLEAR this year I ran the same play at smaller scale: created a phased plan to migrate the production iOS app to Swift 6.2 concurrency and executed it incrementally alongside a six-engineer team.
