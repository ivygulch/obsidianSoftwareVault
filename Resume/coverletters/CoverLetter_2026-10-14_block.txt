To the Bitcoin iOS team at Block,

Your posting says the work is to turn complex financial capabilities into clear, reliable customer experiences, and that the person doing it should own technical direction while writing production code. That is the shape of my last five years. As Principal Software Engineer on Walmart's checkout platform, I was responsible for the checkout flow in the flagship iOS app, which more than five million people use each day — the screen where a customer commits money and has to know, right away and correctly, whether it went through. I owned its technical direction: architecture evolution, modularization, and the long-term maintainability of a shared platform that other teams built on. I wrote and reviewed code in it the whole time.

On foundational systems and initiatives with durable adoption: the checkout platform itself was shared architecture, and the iOS engineering standards I defined for architecture, performance, security, and testing were what a 120+ engineer organization built against across the Walmart, ASDA, and Sam's Club apps. I built the standardized onboarding materials and served as an interview bar-raiser for dozens of candidates a year. On work that was not on a roadmap: my use of AI development tooling at Walmart began as personal experimentation in late 2024, grew into the engineering working groups that evaluated tools for the organization, and ended with me training more than 80 iOS engineers on AI-assisted workflows through live sessions, with more onboarded from the recordings. In the same period I assisted the platform team in evaluating and integrating Builder.io, which engineering management then adopted as the standard for new iOS view work.

On owning features end to end: every new checkout feature launched behind a feature flag, most as A/B experiments that measured customer and business impact, and I owned the analytics that judged them — instrumentation, dashboards, and monitoring through production rollout. The numbers I was accountable for were a 99.9%+ crash-free session rate with roughly 90% of core checkout flows instrumented, held through traffic spikes, and test coverage raised to about 85% through an automated testing discipline that produced a measurable drop in post-release defects. I led release readiness on a large inner-sourced codebase with frequent production releases, collaborated with backend teams on the GraphQL schema and mobile-optimized APIs for checkout and payments, worked regularly with the search and discovery teams on features that spanned app modules, and explored the Android and web codebases to keep iOS checkout aligned across platforms.

On large modular apps with UIKit and Objective-C history: the Walmart platform was unidirectional, reactive design in Swift and Combine on UIKit, modularized with Swift Package Manager. Before that, at Sam's Club, I migrated the Objective-C codebase to Swift with uninterrupted service for roughly two million daily users and led the checkout refactor to a unidirectional, reactive architecture; at ASDA I converted the app to RxSwift. My most recent contract was at CLEAR, the identity-verification company, where I planned and executed a phased migration of the production iOS app to Swift 6 concurrency alongside a six-engineer team and maintained the Fastlane release automation. Beyond iOS: for a volunteer fire department, in a separate solo project, I built a local-first SwiftUI client with Swift 6 concurrency against a Supabase backend I built and operated end to end, and at McGraw-Hill's Sharpen I built an AWS data lake and email, SMS, and push-notification tooling alongside the app's authentication and subscription features.

On AI-fluent engineering: besides the Walmart training, I used Claude Code, Codex, and GitHub Spec Kit across the fire-department and CLEAR work from planning through deployment, and I can walk through how I used the tools for research and development as well as what I accepted and what I rejected or modified. On mentoring and communication: I mentored more than a dozen engineers at Walmart, taught accessibility sessions across the iOS organization, and have presented on testing, architecture, and subscriptions at 360iDev, CocoaConf, and CocoaHeads; the fire-department app started from a non-technical customer's requirements that I turned into specifications.

Two things to be direct about. First, I have not worked with bitcoin, stablecoins, or non-custodial wallets. Payments inside a retail checkout and biometric identity at CLEAR are the nearest high-trust work I have done, and they are not the same thing: I have not built key management, transaction signing, or anything onchain, and I would be learning that domain from your team. Second, on your tooling list: I have not used Bazel or Protocol Buffers in production. My build and dependency work has been Swift Package Manager and Fastlane, my client-server interfaces have been GraphQL and REST, and I have practiced unit, integration, and UI testing at scale but not snapshot testing.

The posting is a Staff role and my last title was Principal. Titles have never decided for me; owning a critical area hands-on is the work I want to do. I am a remote candidate in the US.

I would welcome a conversation about the bitcoin and stablecoin flows on your roadmap and where the iOS architecture needs to go to carry them.

Doug Sjoquist
