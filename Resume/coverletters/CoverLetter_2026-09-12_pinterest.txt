To the Pinterest Search hiring team,

Your posting describes the shopping half of the search problem: queries that carry shopping intent, and the journeys that turn them into something a Pinner actually does. For the last five years I owned the other end of that same journey. As Principal Software Engineer on Walmart's checkout platform, I was responsible for the checkout flow in the flagship iOS app used by more than five million people a day. 

I did not lead or design a search or discovery system, but I worked regularly with those teams at Walmart. Walmart is a heavy user of inner-sourcing, where teams or individuals regularly work with other teams on features that affect multiple modules with the app. I know what happens to conversion when the steps before checkout are wrong. That is the vantage point I would bring to Search, consumer-scale iOS leadership and a product-thinking habit, not ranking or retrieval experience.

Your emphasis on experimentation and data is where the Walmart work maps most directly. Every new checkout feature I led shipped behind a feature flag, and most ran as A/B experiments measuring customer and business impact before full rollout. Building the instrumentation, keeping the team dashboards current, and watching behavior through production rollout was part of leading any feature there, and I treated it as the way decisions got made rather than as reporting. On the API side, I worked with backend teams on the GraphQL schema and the mobile-optimized data-access APIs for checkout and payments, which is the same "robust APIs, data contracts, and experimentation hooks" collaboration you describe. The ML partnership is the piece I have not done; I have not worked with ML teams on model-driven product features, and that would be new ground.

I want to be clear about my multi-platform experience. My leadership has been iOS, but I regularly collaborated with the web and android teams. I explored their codebases regularly to ensure we were aligned, but did not make changes. I defined and championed the engineering standards for architecture, performance, security, and testing across a 120+ engineer iOS organization that ships three brands, Walmart, ASDA, and Sam's Club, on a shared platform, so holding a cohesive experience across surfaces with different product owners is familiar work. I would expect to own the cross-platform front-end direction for Search with strong Android and web leads beside me, and to go deepest on iOS myself.

Your AI interview philosophy is the part of this posting I find most congenial. At Walmart I sat on the working groups that evaluated AI development tools for the iOS organization, contributing evaluation criteria and hands-on assessments, and then trained more than eighty engineers on the workflows that cleared the bar. In 2026 I have practiced what I taught. I built an incident-management app for volunteer fire departments as a separate, solo project, spec-first with GitHub Spec Kit and Claude Code, and validated the iOS client with Swift Testing. At CLEAR, I used Claude Code and Codex to analyze a mature production codebase, draft architectural proposals, and plan and execute a Swift 6.2 concurrency migration alongside a six-engineer team. In each case I can walk you through what how I used the tools for research and development as well as what I accepted and what I rejected or modified from the tools.

Raising the bar across 35+ engineers is the kind of leverage I have exercised through standards, teaching, and hiring rather than headcount: mentoring 12+ engineers, building the onboarding materials the iOS organization used, serving as interview bar-raiser for dozens of candidates a year, and teaching organization-wide sessions on accessibility at the request of the accessibility team. Your "apply technologies developed elsewhere" duty has a concrete precedent as well. I assisted the platform team in evaluating Builder.io, which connected Walmart's design library and Figma to generated iOS views, and engineering management adopted it as the standard for new view work.
