To the Engineering team at First Due,

Earlier this year I built an incident-command and personnel-accountability app for volunteer fire departments, from the first conversation with the customer to a shipped v1.0. It is offline-first: every action writes to local SwiftData storage first, and a sync engine I built reconciles with a Supabase backend when connectivity returns, using a persisted upload queue, a Realtime subscription, last-write-wins conflict resolution, and tombstones so deletions survive reconnect. The map runs on MapKit over an async Core Location service, and the accountability timer survives backgrounding by computing deadlines from wall-clock time and firing a local notification when the phone is locked. I took the customer's non-technical requirements, turned them into formal specifications, and iterated with the customer as the app took shape. Reliable behavior in low-connectivity settings for people whose work depends on it is the problem I have been working on most recently, and I would like to work on it at First Due's scale.

I have been building native iOS apps since 2010, with more than 20 shipped across healthcare, aviation events, training, and consumer domains before and alongside a decade at Walmart. As Principal Software Engineer on the iOS checkout team, I was responsible for the checkout flow in the flagship iOS app serving 5M+ daily active users and owned its shared architecture, which a 120+ engineer organization consumed across multiple apps. I helped establish the iOS engineering standards for architecture, performance, security, and testing, raised checkout test coverage to about 85% with XCTest and XCUITest, and held a 99.9%+ crash-free session rate on the weekly production releases. I led multi-team delivery and release readiness for checkout, drove sprint planning, estimation, and velocity tracking for the team, and worked daily with engineering, product, and QA across US and India time zones. I mentored 12+ engineers, built the standardized iOS onboarding materials, and served as an interview bar-raiser for dozens of candidates a year.

Synchronization and offline work runs through my independent engagements as well. For a physical-therapy prescription app, I architected synchronization between the iOS client, a backend API, and a Realm-backed distributed data layer. For a specialty-pharmacy patient app, I built on Core Data against RESTful APIs and maintained it through multiple App Store releases. At McGraw-Hill I delivered authentication and onboarding features for the Sharpen study app and worked on syncing entitlement state to the backend after purchase or restore, with retry handling.

On the delivery side, at CLEAR this year I maintained and extended the team's Fastlane and GitHub Actions CI/CD configuration, led the migration of the production app to Swift 6 concurrency, did new feature work in SwiftUI with Swift Testing coverage, and assisted the six-engineer team with integrating AI usage into their workflow. For the fire department, in a separate solo project, I used Claude Code and Codex to take the concept from an idea to a working app in a couple of months, and I can walk through how I used the tools for research and development as well as what I accepted and what I rejected or modified.

I have not shipped Android code. At Walmart I was part of the small team tasked with replacing the flagship iOS and Android apps, delivered checkout features to both platforms in parallel with the Android team against shared contracts and specifications, and read the Kotlin codebase regularly to keep iOS checkout aligned, but my implementation work was iOS. iOS is where my depth is and where I would want to stay primary, collaborating at the interface level on Android. I work from Ohio and am set up for a fully remote role.
