Day to Day

As a Senior Manager of Software Engineering, Mobile (iOS), you will manage an engineering team responsible for building scalable, reliable, and high-impact mobile applications that power Indeed's Glassdoor initiatives.

In this role, you will set technical direction, drive execution of large-scale initiatives, and ensure your teams deliver high-quality solutions that improve experiences for job seekers and employers. You will work closely with engineering, product, and QA partners to align priorities, solve complex technical challenges, and deliver meaningful outcomes for the business.

You will also play a key role in developing engineering talent, fostering an innovative technical culture, and shaping long-term technical strategy while ensuring operational excellence across the systems your teams own.
Responsibilities

Manage and grow mobile native engineering teams, supporting career development, performance, and an innovative engineering culture
Define technical direction and guide the design and delivery of scalable, reliable mobile applications on iOS
Partner with product, engineering, and other cross-functional teams to translate business goals into technical solutions
Drive execution of large initiatives across teams, ensuring high quality, reliability, security, and timely delivery
Establish and maintain best-in-class engineering practices, including code quality, system design, testing, and operational excellence
Use data, metrics, and experimentation to inform technical decisions and improve product and system performance
Mentor engineering managers and senior engineers, helping develop the next generation of technical leaders

## Cover letter

Dear Glassdoor hiring team,

The Senior Manager, Software Engineering (Mobile, iOS) role asks for three things that rarely come together in one candidate: deep native iOS engineering, current hands-on expertise in AI-assisted development with the ability to guide teams on it, and a track record of developing engineering talent at scale. That combination is the center of my last decade of work, and I want to be straightforward up front about where my profile matches your posting exactly and where it differs.

On iOS technical direction: I spent 2021–2026 as Principal Software Engineer on the Walmart iOS checkout platform, owning technical strategy — architecture evolution, modularization, and long-term maintainability — for the highest-stakes flow in an app serving millions of daily users. I defined and enforced iOS engineering standards across architecture, performance, security, and testing for the full 120+ engineer iOS organization, established automated testing discipline that raised coverage to roughly 85% with a measurable drop in post-release defects, and maintained 99.9%+ crash-free sessions. Your responsibility line about using data, metrics, and experimentation to inform decisions describes how that team operated: every checkout feature shipped behind feature flags, more than half launched as A/B experiments, and every feature lead owned its instrumentation, dashboards, and production monitoring.

On AI expertise for mobile — the requirement where I can add the most distinctive value: I have been doing exactly the "guide and advise teams" work your posting describes since late 2024. At Walmart I participated in the engineering working groups evaluating AI development tools for the iOS organization, helped the platform team evaluate and integrate Builder.io connecting Walmart's design library with Figma for iOS view generation (subsequently adopted by engineering management as the standard for new iOS view work), and personally trained 80+ iOS engineers on AI-assisted workflows through live sessions. In 2026 I have carried that forward with the current generation of tools: at CLEAR I mentor a six-engineer iOS team on integrating Claude Code and Codex into daily development, and I took a volunteer-fire-department incident-management app from raw customer requirements to a shipped v1.0 solo, built spec-first with agentic tooling across the full lifecycle. I know what these tools do well on iOS codebases, where they fail, and how to move a team from skepticism to productive daily use — because I have done that rollout.

On developing talent: at Walmart I mentored 12+ engineers, built the standardized iOS onboarding materials, served as an iOS interview bar-raiser interviewing dozens of candidates a year, and taught org-wide sessions on accessibility and automated testing. Outside work I have presented at 360iDev, CocoaConf, and CocoaHeads chapters across four cities, co-organized Cincinnati CocoaDev, and taught programming courses as an adjunct instructor. Coaching engineers is not a management duty I picked up late; it has been a constant thread.

One honest note on fit: my recent titles have been Principal and senior IC rather than engineering manager. My leadership at Walmart ran through platform ownership, org-wide standards, hiring, and training rather than direct reports — direct line management in my career is earlier (Assistant Director of Computer Services, managing a university computing staff). If you are looking for a manager whose authority comes from technical credibility, current AI fluency, and a demonstrated coaching practice, that is exactly my profile, and I would welcome a conversation about how I would approach the people-management dimension of the role. I would be glad to walk through the Walmart AI-tooling rollout or the checkout platform work in detail — a 30-minute call is an easy first step.

Sincerely,
Douglas Sjoquist
