Pinterest helps more than 600 million people discover new ideas to help design their life. Our users come to Pinterest to explore ideas and run more than 80 billion search queries every month. Many of these queries represent exploratory search and shopping intent and are broad, which means that the search system should be able to deeply understand this intent, then help people explore content, personalize results, harness visual and multimodal signals effectively, and show the most engaging content up front. All of this means that Pinterest Search presents a unique challenge quite unlike other search systems and the opportunity to innovate on a product that only Pinterest can build.

We are looking for an exceptional Senior Staff Engineer to lead Search's product and front-end strategy across iOS and other platforms such as Android and Web, spanning core product lines like Text Search, Visual Search, and Image Search. In this role, you'll lead several teams carving and evolving the Search experience for Pinterest.




What you'll do:

Own and drive the multi-platform product and front-end strategy for Pinterest Search across iOS, Android, and Web, ensuring a cohesive and delightful experience across Text Search, Visual Search, and Image Search.
Lead the design, implementation, and productionization of end-to-end features and journeys in Search, partnering with product, design, and ML teams to deliver measurable improvements in engagement and quality.
Use AI (including LLMs and other generative techniques) to accelerate exploration and iteration - rapidly prototyping new interaction patterns, drafts of UI/UX variations, and experiment plans - while applying strong engineering judgment and verification to ensure correctness, performance, and quality.
Provide technical leadership across 35+ engineers working on Search front-ends and related product surfaces: define architectural direction, codify best practices, and raise the bar on code quality, performance, and reliability.
Collaborate closely with infrastructure, ML, and back-end teams to design robust APIs, data contracts, and experimentation hooks that enable fast, safe iteration on Search experiences.
Partner with cross-functional teams across Pinterest to identify technologies being developed elsewhere (e.g., new UI systems, ML-driven components, experimentation platforms) and apply them to Search - or extend Search innovations to other teams where they can have outsized impact.
Mentor and grow engineers and technical leaders in the organization, helping them develop strong front-end, full-stack, and product-thinking skills.
What we're looking for:

10+ years of experience building and shipping large-scale, consumer-facing products, with deep expertise in iOS development and/or strong full-stack engineering skills.
Demonstrated experience leading front-end and product strategy across multiple platforms (iOS, Android, Web), including designing architectures that support consistency, performance, and fast iteration.
Strong track record of tech-leading complex, cross-team initiatives from problem definition through execution, using A/B experimentation and data to guide decisions and demonstrate impact.
Experience collaborating with ML, infra, and back-end teams on API design, data flows, and experimentation frameworks that support rich search or discovery experiences.
Demonstrated ability to use AI to improve speed and quality in your workflow (e.g., drafting prototypes, exploring alternative UX flows, synthesizing experiment outcomes), combined with a rigorous approach to validating AI-assisted work through testing, data validation, and peer review.
Excellent communication and collaboration skills, with the ability to influence senior stakeholders and cross-functional partners and align them around a cohesive Search product and front-end vision.
Bachelor's/Master's degree in Computer Science, a related technical field, or equivalent practical experience.

## Cover letter

To the Pinterest Search hiring team,

Your posting describes the shopping half of the search problem: queries that carry shopping intent, and the journeys that turn them into something a Pinner actually does. For the last five years I owned the other end of that same journey. As Principal Software Engineer on Walmart's checkout platform, I was responsible for the flow where intent becomes a purchase in a flagship app used by more than five million people a day. I know what a shopping journey has to carry into its final step, and I know what happens to conversion when the steps before it are wrong. That is the vantage point I would bring to Search. I should be equally clear that I have not built a search or discovery system; what I bring is consumer-scale iOS leadership and a product-thinking habit, not ranking or retrieval experience.

Your emphasis on experimentation and data is where the Walmart work maps most directly. Every new checkout feature I led shipped behind a feature flag, and more than half ran as A/B experiments measuring customer and business impact before full rollout. Building the instrumentation, keeping the team dashboards current, and watching behavior through production rollout was part of leading any feature there, and I treated it as the way decisions got made rather than as reporting. On the API side, I worked with backend teams on the GraphQL schema and the mobile-optimized data-access APIs for checkout and payments, which is the same "robust APIs, data contracts, and experimentation hooks" collaboration you describe. The ML partnership is the piece I have not done; I have not worked with ML teams on model-driven product features, and that would be new ground.

On multi-platform strategy I want to be precise. My platform leadership has been iOS. I defined and championed the engineering standards for architecture, performance, security, and testing across a 120+ engineer iOS organization that ships three brands, Walmart, ASDA, and Sam's Club, on a shared platform, so holding a cohesive experience across surfaces with different product owners is familiar work. I have not led Android or web engineering. I would expect to own the cross-platform front-end direction for Search with strong Android and web leads beside me, and to go deepest on iOS myself.

Your AI interview philosophy is the part of this posting I find most congenial. At Walmart I sat on the working groups that evaluated AI development tools for the iOS organization, contributing evaluation criteria and hands-on assessments, and then trained more than eighty engineers on the workflows that cleared the bar. In 2026 I have practiced what I taught. I built an incident-management app for volunteer fire departments as a separate, solo project, spec-first with GitHub Spec Kit and Claude Code, and validated the iOS client with Swift Testing. At CLEAR, I used Claude Code and Codex to analyze a mature production codebase, draft architectural proposals, and plan and execute a Swift 6.2 concurrency migration alongside a six-engineer team. In every case I can walk you through what the tools produced, what I rejected, and how I verified what I kept, which I gather is exactly what your interviews ask.

Raising the bar across 35+ engineers is the kind of leverage I have exercised through standards, teaching, and hiring rather than headcount: mentoring 12+ engineers, building the onboarding materials the iOS organization used, serving as interview bar-raiser for dozens of candidates a year, and teaching organization-wide sessions on accessibility at the request of the accessibility team. Your "apply technologies developed elsewhere" duty has a concrete precedent as well. I assisted the platform team in evaluating Builder.io, which connected Walmart's design library and Figma to generated iOS views, and engineering management adopted it as the standard for new view work.

One practical matter. The posting is listed as San Francisco / Remote but requires a weekly office day within commuting distance of San Francisco or Palo Alto, and relocation is not offered. I am not within commuting distance. If the remote listing applies to this role, I would like to talk. If the weekly office day is firm, I would rather know now than after we have both invested in the process.

I would welcome a conversation with the hiring manager about where Search's iOS surfaces are today and what a cohesive front-end direction across Text, Visual, and Image Search should look like.
