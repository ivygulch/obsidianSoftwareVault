## Cover letter

Dear Whatnot App Platform team,

App Platform's charter — building the foundation every product pod stands on, and making the "right way" to build also the easiest way — describes the work I did for five years at Walmart more precisely than any posting I've read. As Principal Software Engineer on the iOS checkout platform (2021–2026), I owned platform architecture inside a 120+ iOS engineer organization: I defined and enforced the engineering standards for architecture, performance, security, and testing that every iOS team built against, built the standardized onboarding materials new engineers started from, and mentored 12+ engineers. Checkout was the highest-stakes surface in the app, so the platform-quality bar was concrete: 99.9%+ crash-free sessions, ~90% instrumentation of core flows, and automated test coverage raised to ~85% with a measurable drop in post-release defects.

Two parts of my Walmart work map directly to App Platform's ownership areas. On the design-system side, I assisted the platform team in evaluating and integrating Builder.io — connecting Walmart's design library and Figma to generate and update iOS views — after which engineering management adopted it as the standard for new iOS view work; I then trained 80+ engineers on the workflow through live sessions. That project was exactly the "right way = easiest way" motion: the paved road only wins if it's faster than the dirt path. On the networking side, I collaborated with backend teams on GraphQL schema design and mobile-optimized APIs for checkout and payments.

Your bonus areas are home ground. I've shipped e-commerce iOS continuously since 2014 across Walmart, Sam's Club (~2M DAU), and ASDA (500K+ DAU). At Walmart, every checkout feature I led rolled out behind feature flags — more than half as A/B experiments — and, per the standard responsibility of every feature lead, I built the analytics and telemetry instrumentation, kept the team dashboards current, and monitored behavior through production rollout.

On SwiftUI-driven architecture and shipping speed: my current work is fully on the modern stack. At CLEAR I'm leading the migration of their flagship production app to Swift 6.2 structured concurrency, with all new feature work in SwiftUI and new coverage in Swift Testing, while mentoring their six-engineer iOS team. And earlier this year I took an iOS incident-management app for volunteer fire departments from a non-technical customer's initial requirements to a shipped v1.0 — solo, client and backend both — in about three months. Staying close to users is how I've always worked; a checkout flow rated 4.8 stars across 18 million App Store ratings only gets there by thinking about the customer before the architecture.

I'd welcome a conversation about what App Platform is prioritizing next — stability, the design system, or the persistence and networking layers — and where I could take ownership first. Happy to walk through the Walmart standards work or the Builder.io rollout in as much depth as useful.

Douglas Sjoquist
dwsjoquist@sunetos.com
