At Walmart I was Principal Software Engineer on the iOS checkout platform: the payment, fulfillment, and final-conversion flow in the flagship app, serving 5M+ daily active users. Every new checkout feature shipped behind a feature flag, and more than half launched as A/B experiments measured against customer and business impact; I owned the analytics and telemetry for those features through production rollout, which is where I learned to settle trade-off arguments with data rather than preference. At the request of the engineering accessibility team, I also taught sessions across the entire iOS organization on the reasoning behind accessibility and the iOS techniques for building it in.

The practical-over-elaborate bias you're asking for is one I hold. At CLEAR this year, the plan I built to move the production flagship app to Swift 6.2 concurrency was deliberately phased and implemented incrementally so the six-engineer team kept shipping throughout, and new-feature work moved to SwiftUI in step with the team's modernization direction rather than as a wholesale rewrite. Alongside that, a separate, solo build — an incident-management app for volunteer fire departments — took a non-technical client's requirements to formal specifications and a shipped v1.0: a local-first SwiftUI client using Swift 6.2 concurrency with a Supabase backend.

On the two teams: Member Experience Client Foundations is the closer match to my background. Ads is further from my experience; what transfers there is years of integrating a high-stakes, performance-sensitive flow into a consumer experience without degrading it.

I'd welcome a conversation about either team. I can walk through the Walmart checkout architecture, the GraphQL collaboration, and the CLEAR concurrency migration in whatever depth is useful.
