## Cover letter

Dear Meta iOS Engineering team,

I'm applying for the Software Engineer, iOS role because its responsibilities read like a description of my last five years: I was Principal Software Engineer on the iOS checkout platform at Walmart (2021–2026), owning the architecture, performance, and long-term maintainability of the highest-stakes flow in an app serving millions of daily active users, inside a 120+ iOS engineer organization.

Against your minimum qualifications:

**Data and analysis to explain technical problems.** At Walmart, every feature I led shipped behind feature flags — more than half as A/B experiments measuring customer and business impact before full rollout — and I owned the analytics and telemetry for each: instrumentation, team dashboards, and monitoring through production rollout. Technical decisions at checkout were argued with data because the conversion numbers made everything else noise.

**Driving change within an organization and leading complex technical projects.** I defined and enforced iOS engineering standards — architecture, performance, security, testing — across the full 120+ engineer organization, and led the multi-year testing-discipline effort that raised checkout coverage to roughly 85% with a measurable drop in post-release defects. In 2025 I assisted the platform team in evaluating and integrating Builder.io, connecting Walmart's design library in Figma to iOS view generation; engineering management adopted it as the standard for new view work, and I trained 80+ engineers on the workflows. Organizational change that sticks needs standards, tooling, and training together — I've driven all three.

**8+ years of programming experience.** Fifteen-plus years of production iOS alone, from Objective-C through Swift 6.2, on top of an earlier career in Java/J2EE systems.

Against the preferred qualifications, three points worth calling out specifically:

**Large-scale production, testable codebases.** Beyond Walmart checkout, I migrated Sam's Club's Objective-C codebase to Swift with uninterrupted service for roughly two million daily active users and led its checkout refactor to unidirectional reactive architecture — the kind of large-effort technical-debt reduction this role names. Maintained 99.9%+ crash-free sessions on Walmart checkout.

**Multithreading and memory management.** My current engagement at CLEAR has me leading the migration of their flagship production iOS app to Swift 6.2 structured concurrency — async/await, actors, Sendable, strict concurrency checking — which is multithreading correctness work end to end: finding the data races the compiler now surfaces, deciding isolation boundaries, and mentoring the six-engineer team through the model.

**AI tools with measurable impact, and ongoing AI skill development.** This is where my last two years differentiate. The Walmart Builder.io work above was AI-assisted view generation adopted org-wide. Since early 2026 I've built AI-native as daily practice: I took an incident-management app for volunteer fire departments from a non-technical customer's requirements to shipped v1.0 as sole engineer using GitHub Speckit for specification generation and Claude Code for implementation, and at CLEAR I use Claude Code and Codex across codebase research, architectural proposals, implementation, and test development — and mentor the iOS team on doing the same. I apply these tools across planning, specification, implementation, testing, verification, and deployment, as accelerators for engineering judgment rather than substitutes for it.

I should be direct that my AI-governance experience is practitioner-level — quality and accuracy review of AI-generated code as part of engineering standards — rather than formal risk-assessment or bias-mitigation program work.

The role is listed Remote, US, which fits how I've worked for years. I'd welcome a conversation about which team this req maps to and where an engineer with platform-ownership and AI-adoption depth would land — happy to go deep on the Walmart checkout architecture, the Swift 6 concurrency migration, or the AI tooling rollout.

Douglas Sjoquist
dwsjoquist@sunetos.com
