I worked with Walmart's backend teams on the GraphQL schema and mobile-optimized APIs for checkout and payments, collaborated regularly with the search and discovery teams through the inner-sourcing model on features that spanned multiple app modules, and led multi-team delivery and release readiness on a large inner-sourced codebase with frequent production releases. I also explored the Android and web codebases regularly to keep the iOS implementation aligned, though I did not make changes to them.

On roadmaps where short-term work ladders to the long-term direction: the checkout platform was a multi-year arc. The architectural patterns I established on the small initial team during the 2020 ground-up rewrite of the Walmart app became the foundation I then owned and evolved as Principal from 2021 to 2026, with each year's feature delivery laddering into the modularization and maintainability direction. At CLEAR this year I ran the same play at smaller scale: created a phased plan to migrate the production iOS app to Swift 6.2 concurrency and executed it incrementally alongside a six-engineer team.

I can walk you through how I use the tools for research and development as well as what I accept and what I reject or modify.

Three things to be direct about. I have not built iOS media infrastructure; my platform depth is transactional — checkout, payments, and real-time reliability under traffic spikes — not media pipelines. My influence ran to engineering management and organization leaders, whose adoption of the Builder.io standard and the iOS engineering standards came on recommendations I helped drive, but I have not worked day-to-day with executive stakeholders at the VP-and-above level. And my responsible-AI experience is the practical kind — evaluation criteria, hands-on quality and accuracy assessment, and judging what tool output to accept or reject — not formal risk-assessment or bias-mitigation programs.
