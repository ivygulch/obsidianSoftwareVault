Full job description
iOS Engineer - Sponsorships
Substack is building a new economic engine for culture, giving the brightest, most interesting, and most creative people on the internet the power of their own publishing platform. The terms of our culture should not be set by gate-keeping legacy media or chaos-fueling social media, but by the people who actually make and participate in that culture. Substack's model, based on direct subscriptions, has fueled an explosion of independent publishing. It empowers creators with economic autonomy, creative ownership, and a direct connection to their most engaged audiences.


About the Sponsorships Team
Our team's goal is to augment our existing direct subscriptions model with paid sponsorships. Brands are interested in connecting with Substack creators, and Substack creators are interested in making more money, but we want to support this behavior in a way that doesn't degrade the user experience or undermine trust in the platform.
Everyone contributes to the product direction. Our team regularly brainstorms directly with leadership, and every engineer contributes ideas for new products and experiences. We also sometimes work directly with our Sponsorship partners (e.g. Polymarket) to develop new experiences.
We're a small team of generalists, so we especially value an interest in working across our tech stack, willingness to prototype, and product-mindedness. You'd be the iOS leader on this team, so code review, mentorship and thoughtful technical architecting will also be key pieces of this role.

Responsibilities

Develop new and existing sponsorship experiences in the core iOS app for creators and their audiences.

Contribute to high level product and brainstorming discussions around sponsorship experiences.

Iterate on key flows in the Substack mobile apps and web platform, and run A/B tests.

Collaborate with designers, writers, support staff, and many others. From product concept and research to implementation, experimentation, and measurement, we expect you to own your work and deliver high-quality solutions.

Work directly with customers to solve their problems. The entire company (CEO included) participates in customer support in order to drive empathy for our users and help us build better products.

Our team also works directly with Sponsorship partners (we often say "build-with" partners) to gather product ideas and find solutions that work for both brands and creators.

Requirements

At least 3+ years of software engineering experience.

Experience shipping high quality, native iOS apps to the App Store. Bonus for both SwiftUI and UIKit.

Independent and autonomous. We're too small to micromanage, and expect that every person at the company owns their work and can be a leader.

Hold yourself and others to a high standard when working on production systems.

Take pride in building elegant and delightful product experiences. Substack works best when it gets out of the way so creators can focus on writing and connecting with their readers.

Enjoy collaboration with a diverse group of thinkers while bringing our own unique experience and background to the team.

Believe in Substack's mission to build a better business model for writing.

Experience at a fast-growing startup or working on a high-performing growth team is not required, but is a plus.

## Cover letter

To the Substack Sponsorships team,

The constraint in your posting is the part I read twice: add sponsorships to a subscription product without degrading the experience or the trust readers place in it. That is the discipline I worked under for the last five years. As Principal Software Engineer on Walmart's checkout platform, I was responsible for the checkout flow in the flagship iOS app, which more than five million people use each day. Every new checkout feature shipped behind a feature flag, with most run as A/B experiments measuring customer and business impact, and I owned the analytics that judged them: instrumentation, dashboards, and monitoring through production rollout. Checkout is a flow where a small loss of trust shows up in the numbers within days, and I learned to design and measure for it.

On shipping native iOS: I have shipped to the App Store continuously since 2010, from twenty-plus independent client apps to Walmart's flagship app, which is rated 4.8 stars on the App Store from millions of ratings, with the checkout UI among my direct contributions. UIKit with Swift and Combine was the primary framework at Walmart; SwiftUI is where my recent work sits. At CLEAR this year I used SwiftUI for new feature work on the flagship app, planned and executed a phased migration of the production app to Swift 6.2 concurrency, and maintained the six-engineer team's Fastlane CI/CD configuration. For a volunteer fire department, in a separate solo app, I built a local-first SwiftUI client with a Supabase backend from a non-technical customer's requirements to a shipped 1.0, with Swift Testing coverage across the client, and iterated directly with the client and the department's volunteers.

On being the iOS leader for a small team of generalists: at Walmart I defined the engineering standards for architecture, performance, security, and testing that a 120+ engineer iOS organization built against across the Walmart, ASDA, and Sam's Club apps. I mentored more than a dozen engineers, built the standardized onboarding materials, and served as an interview bar-raiser. Code review and architectural direction were daily work, and so was shipping features on the platform I owned. Across the stack, I worked with Walmart's backend teams on the GraphQL schema and mobile-optimized APIs for checkout and payments, built and operated the Supabase backend for the fire-department app, and at McGraw-Hill built internal tools including an AWS data lake and push notifications alongside the iOS work. I explored the Android and web codebases regularly at Walmart to keep the iOS implementation aligned, though I did not make changes to them.

On product-mindedness and prototyping: at TherexRx I was the sole iOS developer on a physical-therapy prescription app and implemented its auto-renewable subscriptions with RevenueCat and StoreKit, taking them through App Store review. At McGraw-Hill's Sharpen study app I worked on the paywall and purchase and restore flows as part of the product team. Both meant sitting with the people who owned the product and turning what they wanted into working software, which is also how the fire-department app went from a conversation to a release.

On AI: I sat on Walmart's working groups that evaluated AI development tools for the iOS organization and trained more than eighty engineers on the workflows that cleared the bar. I use Claude Code and Codex daily. I can walk you through how I use the tools for research and development as well as what I accept and what I reject or modify.

Two things to be direct about. I have not built sponsorship or advertising products; my monetization work is checkout at Walmart and in-app subscriptions at Sharpen and TherexRx, the subscription side of the model you are augmenting. And I have not worked with brand partners as a build-with counterpart; the nearest experience is coordinating schema compatibility and release timing with a parallel co-vendor's app at BioRx, and integrating third-party platforms such as RevenueCat where their roadmap shaped ours.

The mission line is not a formality for me. I read Substack as a subscriber before I looked at it as an employer: I follow a number of writers there, and what keeps me reading is a clean, low-distraction environment where long-form work has room. I also like where the control sits, with the writer owning the relationship and the email list. Adding sponsorships without giving up either of those properties is the constraint I would want to work under.

The posting is titled iOS Engineer and my last title was Principal. Titles have never decided for me; being the hands-on iOS lead on a small product team, with architecture and mentoring responsibility, is what I want to do. The posting does not state a location. I am a remote candidate in the US, not within commuting distance of San Francisco, and open to periodic visits if the role is hybrid.

I would welcome a conversation with the hiring manager about where sponsorship experiences sit in the app today and what the first experiments on the roadmap look like.

Doug Sjoquist
