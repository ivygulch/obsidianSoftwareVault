About us

Office Hours is an on-demand expert network that connects leading organizations with trusted experts across various knowledge domains. Experts earn income by sharing their knowledge through advisory work, projects, and AI model training. Our platform handles the complexities behind the scenes— screening, compliance, scheduling, and payments—so knowledge sharing stays focused on meaningful insights and real impact.

We're a hyper-growth and profitable company, quickly expanding our expert network, launching new offices, and new products. We are headquartered in San Francisco, with offices in Brooklyn and Bangalore. Our customers include the fastest-growing digital health companies, technology companies, institutional investment firms, consulting firms and AI Labs. We are backed by top marketplace investors and operators of companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, Affirm.

What we believe

Human knowledge is the world's most valuable asset. And yet, despite being more interconnected than ever, most knowledge still remains stuck in our heads, inaccessible and underutilized. Our vision is to make human knowledge easily accessible and infinitely scalable by building tools for the new age knowledge economy.

About the role

We're hiring our first dedicated iOS engineer to build a mobile experience that puts Office Hours in the hands of experts everywhere. This is a true ground-floor opportunity: you'll shape the app itself, from its architecture to its craft to how it feels to use, and help grow a mobile team from here.

You'll have real ownership from the start, making the foundational technical and product decisions that shape how the app is built and where it goes.

We're looking for a deeply product-minded engineer who cares about user experience as much as code. You have a real eye for polish and interaction detail, you sweat the small things that make an app feel effortless, and you partner naturally with design and product to get there.

We build with AI coding tools, and we want someone who's leaning into them, using them to prototype, iterate, and ship faster without compromising on craft.

## Cover letter

Dear Office Hours team,

Being someone's first dedicated iOS engineer is not hypothetical for me — it's what I did from February to May of this year. A client came to me with nothing but requirements in his head for FirefighterSpot, an incident-management app for volunteer fire departments, and I took it to a shipped v1.0 as the sole engineer: architecture, state, navigation, offline (a local-first SwiftUI client with cross-device sync), the Supabase backend behind it, and delivery. That is the end-to-end ownership your posting describes, exercised recently, on modern Swift 6.2 concurrency and SwiftUI — your exact stack.

And your bonus line about "agentic coding tools such as Claude Code or Codex" describes my daily workflow by name. FirefighterSpot was built spec-first, with GitHub Speckit generating specifications and Claude Code driving implementation. On my current engagement at CLEAR, I use Claude Code and Codex across codebase research, architectural proposals, implementation, and test development, and I mentor their six-engineer iOS team on making agentic tooling part of everyday work. Before the tools had these names, I helped drive AI-assisted development adoption at Walmart — evaluating and integrating Builder.io's Figma-to-iOS-view generation with the platform team and training 80+ iOS engineers as it became the org standard. I lean into these tools exactly the way you describe: to raise velocity and quality together, with engineering judgment staying firmly in charge.

On your other asks:

**Evolve vs. rebuild judgment.** I've made this call in both directions across a career of it: incremental Objective-C-to-Swift migration with uninterrupted service for roughly two million daily active users at Sam's Club (evolve), the complete ground-up rewrite of the Walmart flagship app as part of the small initial team (rebuild), and the phased Swift 6.2 structured-concurrency migration I'm currently leading through CLEAR's production codebase (evolve, carefully). The judgment about which is right is precisely what five years owning Walmart's checkout platform architecture trains.

**Product partnership and craft.** The primary focus of my CLEAR engagement is enhancing the user experience of their flagship consumer app — UX work is the deliverable, not a side effect. And on "apps users genuinely love": the Walmart iOS app holds a 4.8-star App Store rating across 18 million ratings, and I was a key part of the UI development for its checkout component — the screen where user patience is thinnest and craft matters most. At Walmart I also worked design-system-adjacent daily (the Builder.io/Figma integration) and taught accessibility implementation across the entire iOS organization, because polish that excludes users isn't polish.

**Standards, tooling, and practices as the team grows.** I've built this at both scales you'll pass through: engineering standards, onboarding materials, and interview practices for a 120+ engineer iOS organization at Walmart, and hands-on CI/CD ownership (Fastlane) plus mentorship on a six-engineer team at CLEAR. I know what's worth formalizing at each stage and what's premature.

I'll also say plainly: your mission lands with me personally. I've spent decades on the expert-knowledge-sharing side myself — conference sessions at 360iDev and CocoaConf, years of CocoaHeads talks, fourteen years as an adjunct instructor. A platform that makes that kind of knowledge accessible and lets experts earn from it is a product I'd genuinely use from both sides.

The role's shape — fully remote, deep ownership, ground floor — fits how I work best. I'd welcome a conversation about where the app is today and what the first ninety days should ship; I'm happy to walk through the FirefighterSpot build end to end, since it's the closest thing to a rehearsal for this role that exists.

Douglas Sjoquist
dwsjoquist@sunetos.com
