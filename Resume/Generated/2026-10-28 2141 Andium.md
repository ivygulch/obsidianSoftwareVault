## Cover letter

To the mobile engineering team at Andium,

An app that helps field crews resolve a methane event in real time has to keep working at a well site where connectivity is poor and the person holding the phone is busy. That is the kind of app I shipped most recently. Earlier this year I built an incident-command and personnel-accountability app for volunteer fire departments, from the first conversation with the customer to a shipped v1.0 and a 1.0.1 maintenance release. It is offline-first: every action writes to local storage first, and a sync engine I built reconciles with the backend when connectivity returns, with a persisted upload queue, live updates over a Realtime subscription, last-write-wins conflict resolution, and tombstones so deletions survive reconnect. The accountability timer survives backgrounding by computing deadlines from wall-clock time and firing a local notification when the phone is locked. I took the customer's non-technical requirements, turned them into formal specifications before writing code, and iterated with the customer as the app took shape. I would like to bring that to Andium's iOS platform.

I have been building native iOS apps since 2010, with more than 20 shipped to the App Store across healthcare, aviation events, training, and consumer domains before and alongside a decade at Walmart. As Principal Software Engineer on the iOS checkout team, I was responsible for the checkout flow in the flagship iOS app serving 5M+ daily active users and owned its shared architecture, which a 120+ engineer organization consumed across multiple apps. I helped establish the iOS engineering standards for architecture, performance, security, and testing, raised checkout test coverage to about 85% with XCTest and XCUITest across unit, integration, and UI tests, and held a 99.9%+ crash-free session rate on the weekly production releases. I drove sprint planning, estimation, and velocity tracking for the checkout team and worked daily with engineering, product, and QA across US and India time zones, so detailed estimates and delivering against them were a standing part of the job.

On Swift, SwiftUI, UIKit, and concurrency: the Walmart platform was unidirectional, reactive design in Swift and Combine on UIKit, modularized with Swift Package Manager. At CLEAR this year I planned and executed a phased migration of the production iOS app to Swift 6 concurrency alongside a six-engineer team, did new feature work in SwiftUI with Swift Testing coverage, and maintained and extended the team's Fastlane and GitHub Actions CI/CD configuration. The fire-department app is SwiftUI with Swift 6 strict concurrency throughout, structured as 16 Swift packages, and covered by more than 400 Swift Testing tests plus an XCUITest flow.

On REST APIs, Core Data, and the rest of your plus list: for a specialty-pharmacy patient app I built on Core Data against RESTful APIs and maintained it through multiple App Store releases, and for a physical-therapy prescription app I architected synchronization between the iOS client, a backend API, and a distributed data layer and took its auto-renewable subscriptions through App Store review. I migrated the Sam's Club Objective-C codebase to Swift without interrupting service for roughly two million daily users and rewrote ASDA's legacy Objective-C components in Swift. At McGraw-Hill, alongside iOS feature work, I built internal tools including an AWS data lake and push notifications for the mobile apps. At Walmart I taught accessibility sessions across the iOS organization, which is where Apple's interface guidelines get tested against a very large user base.

Two gaps to be direct about. I have not used Core ML in a shipped app; the nearest work is the fire-department app's VisionKit scanner for QR unit transfer, and I would be learning Core ML on the job. I have not worked in oil and gas. The closest I have come to an industrial operator is six years as architect and lead developer of a quality-management system for a platinum and palladium mining company, integrated with its JD Edwards ERP for shipment intake and assay tracking, so I know what it is like to build software that operations staff depend on, but I do not know flare stacks or compressors and would need your team to teach me the equipment.
