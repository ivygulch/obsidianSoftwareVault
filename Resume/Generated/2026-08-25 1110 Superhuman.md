Superhuman is building the AI productivity platform that works everywhere people do, and Superhuman Mail is central to how people communicate and get work done. Today, we’re the fastest email experience in the world - and one our customers truly love.

On the Superhuman Mail team, you will own our team’s outcomes for our iOS mobile roadmap alongside the Engineering Manager and Product Manager. ## Cover letter

To the Superhuman Mail team,

Two responsibilities in this posting describe the work I have actually been doing for the past two years: owning the technical vision for a mobile platform, and leading a team into an AI-native way of working. That combination is rare enough that I want to make the case directly.

You ask for fluency with AI coding tools and a point of view on how they change the way teams build software. Mine was formed across three very different contexts. At Walmart, I participated in the engineering working groups evaluating AI development tools for a 120+ engineer iOS organization and trained 80+ engineers on AI-assisted workflows through live sessions. In early 2026, on an independent, solo engagement, I took an incident-management app for volunteer fire departments from a non-technical customer's requirements to a shipped v1.0 — GitHub Spec Kit driving spec-first development, Claude Code as the implementation partner, SwiftUI client with a Supabase backend I built and operated. And at CLEAR, I worked with a six-engineer iOS team on integrating Claude Code and Codex into their day-to-day workflow while modernizing their production app to Swift 6.2 concurrency. The point of view that came out of it: these tools move the bottleneck from writing code to specifying and verifying it. Teams that treat them as accelerators for engineering judgment — investing in specification quality, test discipline, and review — ship dramatically faster without giving up quality. Teams that treat them as substitutes for judgment ship rework. Leading a mobile team into that first posture is exactly the enablement work I did at Walmart and CLEAR.

The platform-vision half is my Walmart record. As Principal Software Engineer for checkout, I owned iOS technical strategy — architecture evolution, modularization, long-term maintainability — for a flagship app serving 5M+ daily active users, and defined engineering standards across the iOS organization. Checkout is where the speed-versus-reliability tradeoff you describe lives: every new feature I led shipped behind a feature flag, more than half as A/B experiments measuring customer and business impact, with analytics I instrumented and monitored through rollout, all while holding 99.9%+ crash-free sessions. Fast and reliable as you scale is a practiced discipline for me, not an aspiration.

On 0-to-1 work and operating with limited guidance: beyond the fire-department app, I was a core contributor on the small initial team for the ground-up rewrite of the Walmart iOS app, and I shipped 20+ iOS apps as an independent developer before that. The full-stack plus applies as well — I own backend work when the iOS product needs it (Supabase for the fire-department app; a decade as a Java/J2EE architect before iOS). On mentoring: at Walmart I mentored 12+ engineers, built the organization's standardized iOS onboarding materials, served as an interview bar-raiser, and taught accessibility sessions across the entire iOS engineering organization.

I have not built an email client. What I bring to Mail is the layer underneath: platform architecture that keeps a mobile app fast as it grows, product-minded delivery habits, and a working method for making a small team dramatically more productive with AI tooling. I would welcome a conversation about where the Mail iOS roadmap is headed and where the one-to-two-year platform investments need to land — and I am happy to walk through the spec-first process or the CLEAR modernization work in detail on a call.
