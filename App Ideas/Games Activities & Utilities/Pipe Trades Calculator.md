# Pipe Trades Calculator

**One-liner:** An offline, buy-once pipefitting calculator and dimension reference for the job site.
**Target user:** Pipefitters, steamfitters, and pipe welders, including apprentices; about 504,500 plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters in the US (BLS; the pipefitter share is unverified).
**Problem:** The standard app, Pipe Trades Pro, has been subscription-only at $39.99 a year since April 2022. Of its 39 one- and two-star reviews since then, 36 are about pricing. The one-time-price apps are stale since 2015–2018 or have under 220 ratings.
**Differentiator:** One-time purchase, fully offline with no entitlement check, feet-inch-fraction entry, and the calculations no current app covers: grooved and threaded take-offs with thread makeup, saddle and lateral layouts.

## The Three Questions

**Who pays, how often, how much?**
A working fitter or apprentice, once. Reviewers name $25–$40 as fair, or the handheld calculator's price ($35–$70). The newest rival charges $12.99. Estimated range, net: under $100 a month (low), $570–$1,400 (mid), $4,300–$8,500 (high, needs QuickBend-level adoption). All estimates.

**Year-2 maintenance cost:**
One pass per iOS release, table corrections from user reports, occasional standards revisions, email support. No server, no content feed.

**Kill criteria:**
- PipeBook (the $12.99 one-time app released August 2026) sustains more than about 100 ratings a year.
- Calculated Industries restores a one-time price for Pipe Trades Pro.
- ASME declines permission to republish dimension tables and a legal read says the tables cannot be used without it.
- A working fitter who reviews a prototype finds nothing it does that the existing apps do not.

## Concept

Simple, rolling, and parallel offsets returning travel and cut length net of take-offs and weld gap; odd-angle elbow cuts and miters; tables for pipe schedules, butt-weld take-offs, and flanges with stud count and length; large bore; metric switch; iPad and landscape layouts; diagrams for every function. The machinist feeds-and-speeds market could reuse the app shell but not the data, and its proven demand is about one-sixth as large.

## Prior Art

- **Pipe Trades Pro (Calculated Industries)** — 4,330 ratings at 4.81, subscription-only. The trade standard, taught in union training centers. Ratings grew from 3,251 to 4,330 in 20 months, so the subscription is not hurting it.
- **PipeBook Pipefitter Calculator** — $12.99 one-time, offline, solo US developer, released 2026-08-06, 10 ratings. Already holds the buy-once positioning.
- **QuickBend (conduit bending)** — $6.99, 3,969 ratings, individual seller, #1 on Productivity paid. The model of a loved trade app; adds 622 ratings a year.
- **Piping DataBase – XTREME, Pipe Fitter Calculator, Easy Offset** — one-time apps last updated 2015–2018, still charting on small paid charts.

## Why It Could Work

- Demand is live and measured from archived rating snapshots.
- Users state the price they would pay.
- No backend, no content treadmill, no regulated liability.
- By rating count the leaders are 2.7 to 10 times larger on iOS than on Google Play.

## Why It Might Not

- The one-time-price wedge was taken two months ago.
- 23 pipe-trade apps were released in 2026; 20 have zero to three ratings.
- The incumbent owns the union training-center channel.
- No one-time pipe app has passed 214 US ratings in 16 years.
- Some plants and refineries ban phones.
- Republishing ASME tables needs written permission; no legal review was done.

## Open Questions

- Does any incumbent hold an ASME licence, and what does permission cost?
- How do fitters actually find apps: search, a journeyman's recommendation, or the training center?
- What do sales look like behind PipeBook's first ten ratings?
