# Chief Interview — First-Level Questions

Purpose: high-level questions for the chief if the department chooses the custom-app path. Each is phrased to be answerable by a non-technical chief, and each answer determines which second-level questions (largely already drafted in [[Discovery Questions]]) are worth asking. Ask these before any feature talk.

## 1. What's making this worth doing now?

The PC system has presumably been inadequate for a while — what changed, or what keeps going wrong? A missed event, a staffing surprise, a new officer pushing for it?

_Second level this opens: the specific breakdowns to fix first, who feels the pain most (members vs. admins), and whether there's a date this needs to work by (training season, election of officers, budget cycle)._

## 2. Walk me through a typical month of department activity.

What kinds of events happen — trainings, business meetings, work details, fundraisers, standbys? Who decides them, who announces them, and how do members hear about them today?

_Second level: event types and their differences (visibility, required gear, attachments), recurring vs. one-off entry, how changes and cancellations propagate, and who counts as an "admin."_

## 3. When members answer committed / possible / can't make it — what do you do with that information?

What decision does the count drive? Calling more people, cancelling, confirming a minimum crew? Is "who hasn't answered" as important as who has?

_Second level: headcount and roster views, minimum-staffing warnings, nudging non-responders, response cutoffs, and whether actual attendance needs recording afterward (LOSAP points, training credit, participation requirements)._

## 4. Who is the least tech-comfortable member this has to work for?

Describe them. What phone do they carry, if any? Would they install an app?

_Second level: iPhone/Android mix (major cost/scope fork), text or email fallback, how members get access and who removes it when someone leaves, and whether the station PC stays part of the picture._

## 5. What has to exist on paper or on a wall?

Does a monthly calendar get printed and posted at the station or carried in trucks today? Should it continue to?

_Second level: print formats (month grid, event rosters), the station-PC or wall-display role, and whether truck computers matter at all in the new system._

## 6. Does anything downstream depend on this calendar?

Reports to the township or county, training-hour records, LOSAP, grant paperwork? Even if the app doesn't manage records, does someone retype calendar information into something else?

_Second level: exports (even a simple spreadsheet), end-of-year totals, and a clear line between what the app owns and what stays in existing systems — this is where "calendar only" scope either holds or quietly grows._

## 7. A year in: who's running it?

Who adds members, fixes a wrong event, answers "how do I" questions? Is that person an officer, Jason, or someone else — and what happens when that person's term ends?

_Second level: admin succession, department vs. personal ownership of accounts (Apple developer account, hosting), and expectations for changes and support after v1.0 — the fixed-scope boundary._

## 8. What would make you consider this a failure a year from now?

Has the department tried tools before that didn't stick? Why didn't they?

_Second level: adoption risks by member segment, what "everyone uses it" realistically means, and what the rollout needs (a demo night, a trial with officers first, running both systems in parallel for a month)._

## Notes

_Questions 3, 4, and 8 matter most for scope: the RSVP-decision question defines the core feature, the least-tech-member question defines platform scope, and the failure question surfaces the adoption constraints no feature list captures. Question 6 is the scope-creep tripwire — the stated "calendar only" scope holds only if nothing downstream depends on it._
