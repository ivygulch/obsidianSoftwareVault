# Department Calendar Options

Prepared by Doug Sjoquist · August 25, 2026

## The need

In the department's words:

> "A calendar app for our department only. Not something for a broader audience. Private to us with monthly events, reminders, possible attendee status (committed, possible, can't make it) — accessible to members and editable from phone by admins."

That's the whole scope — no records management or other advanced features. Today the department's only system is an older PC-based one, reachable from the station PC and some truck computers, with nothing on members' phones.

Before deciding on a custom-built app, here is what already exists for exactly this need.

## Option A — Free group apps built for this

These are mature, heavily used, phone-first apps for private groups. Any of them could be running this week at zero or trivial cost.

| | BAND | Spond | TeamSnap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free | Free | ~$120/yr (RSVP tracking is a paid feature) |
| Private to the department | Yes — invite-only groups | Yes | Yes |
| Monthly events + recurring | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Reminders | Yes | Yes, incl. nudging non-responders | Yes |
| Three-state RSVP (committed / possible / can't) | RSVP yes; "maybe" status needs hands-on confirmation | **No** — accept/decline only | **Yes / No / Maybe — the closest match** |
| Members view, admins edit from phone | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Printable monthly calendar | **Yes — built-in Print on the PC website** (pick calendar, view, period) | No — sync to a phone/Google calendar and print there | Browser-print of the web month view |
| Phone-calendar sync | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Track record | 50M+ users; 62K App Store ratings (4.7) | 3.5M+ users; 4.85 App Store rating | 25M users claimed; 59K ratings (4.76); ads on free tier |

**Read on the three**: TeamSnap matches the three-state RSVP exactly but costs ~$120/yr and shows ads. BAND is free, prints a real monthly calendar (useful for the station wall and trucks), and likely covers the RSVP need — worth an hour of hands-on checking. Spond misses the "possible" status, which is central to the ask. Support for all three is self-service help centers — no one to call.

**Google Calendar** is the fourth free option: Yes/No/Maybe invites and the best month-grid printing of anything here, but it requires everyone to live in Google accounts and has no roster or headcount view — workable only if the department already runs on Google.

## Option B — Fire-department platforms (beyond this scope)

For completeness: subscription platforms built for fire departments — IamResponding (from ~$349/yr, the in-domain standard, centered on dispatch alerting), Responserack ($300–720/yr for small departments, volunteer-department records), and Station Boss (from $1,800/yr, all-in-one operations). All include an events calendar, but the calendar is a side module of a records/alerting system the department has said it doesn't need. None document the three-state RSVP; none document monthly-calendar printing.

Only two reasons to look here: if the department **already subscribes to IamResponding** for alerting (many volunteer departments do — the calendar would already be paid for), or if the scope later grows into incident/training records. Otherwise these are more system and more money than the ask.

## Option C — A custom-built app

A purpose-built app matched exactly to the ask: private to the department, the exact committed / possible / can't-make-it flow, reminders the way the department wants them, printable monthly calendar included, and room to grow only if the department ever wants it. The trade-offs, stated plainly:

- **Cost shape**: the free apps are $0 forever; a custom build is a real upfront investment (department-funded) with small ongoing hosting costs — and no per-year licensing.
- **Time**: a free app runs this week; a custom v1.0 is a matter of months.
- **Fit**: the free tools each miss something — Spond the "possible" status, TeamSnap the price/ads, BAND possibly the RSVP granularity. A custom app misses nothing in the ask.
- **Continuity**: the department would depend on its developer for changes, the flip side of owning the system.

My recommendation: trial the closest free option first — BAND or TeamSnap, one month of real events with a handful of members. If the RSVP flow and day-to-day use hold up, the free app wins on every practical measure. If members hit real friction — the RSVP granularity, the extra clutter of a general-purpose social app, admin annoyances — then a custom build is justified, and the chief will be choosing it over a known alternative rather than in the dark. I'm glad to build it if that's where the department lands.

## Suggested next steps

1. Hands-on check of BAND (free): set up a private group, enter one month of real events, confirm what the RSVP statuses actually look like.
2. In parallel, confirm whether the department or mutual-aid partners already run IamResponding — if so, look at its calendar before anything else.
3. Run the trial month with a handful of members (include at least one admin editing from a phone).
4. Reconvene: free app suffices, or scope the custom build against what the trial exposed.
