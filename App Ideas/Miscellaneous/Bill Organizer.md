# Bill Organizer

**One-liner:** A manual bill calendar with reminders, totals, and paid history, with no bank login.
**Target user:** People who track bills by hand and do not want to link accounts. Reviewers of the incumbent describe themselves as a scatterbrain, a senior citizen and caregiver, and someone recovering from a stroke.
**Problem:** Bills Organizer & Reminder (24,715 ratings) has had no release since April 2024 and fails to launch intermittently on recent iOS versions; its 100 most recent reviews average 3.76 against 4.69 lifetime. No rival covers the repeated complaints together: sync that survives a phone change, CSV export, partner sharing, and no bill cap sprung after data entry.
**Differentiator:** Opens reliably; unlimited bills before any paywall prompt; iCloud sync and partner sharing; complete recurrence rules; mark-as-paid from the notification; CSV export; one-time price.

## The Three Questions

**Who pays, how often, how much?**
A household bill-payer, once. One-time unlocks of $4–$10 are the norm and are praised by name; "free" is a suggested modifier on every head search term. Estimated range, net: about $70 a month (low), about $450 (mid), about $2,500 (high, which needs the incumbent's search position). All estimates, and the lowest of the five finalists.

**Year-2 maintenance cost:**
One compatibility pass per iOS release, StoreKit and widget API changes, and support mail on sync and lost data. No content, no server. Support is unforgiving: a missed reminder or lost history produces one-star reviews in every app in this category.

**Kill criteria:**
- The incumbent ships a compatibility release.
- Apple extends the Wallet Recurring view beyond Apple Card, or Reminders gains amounts and a paid history.
- A search-rank test shows no path past the incumbent on the head terms, where it still ranks first on six of eight.

## Concept

Upcoming list, calendar view, monthly total, paid history, and an optional income line for "left after bills". Recurrence covering weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, monthly, every-N-days, quarterly, and annual, with edit-one-or-all-future. iPad layout, widgets, Siri through App Intents. Never moves money or links accounts, which keeps it clear of regulated liability.

## Prior Art

- **Bills Organizer & Reminder** — 24,715 ratings, stale since April 2024, still first for most head terms; 59 of its recent 100 reviews are five-star, so it is degraded, not dead.
- **Bills Manager** — 8,079 ratings, healthy, shipped within the last eight weeks; its worst review theme is losing data on a phone change.
- **Bill Tracker Pro** — 5,014 ratings, $18–$50 a year; its switch to subscription drew 22 low reviews in three months.
- **Chronicle** — subscription at $14.99 a year; shipped Siri support in September 2026.
- **Rocket Money, Monarch, YNAB** — the bank-linked adjacent category; the reason a no-login app is a distinct need.

## Why It Could Work

- Apple's own search suggestions include "bill tracking apps similar to bills organizer &".
- The simplest build of the five, with no backend, content, licence, or regulatory exposure.
- One-time pricing is what this audience asks for.

## Why It Might Not

- One compatibility release from the incumbent closes the gap.
- 60 bill apps released in 2025–2026 appear in the top results; none has reached 100 ratings.
- Apple is moving into the job with iOS 27 Reminders and Wallet.
- The incumbent's review flow is inflated by daily rating prompts, so its demand signal is overstated.
- The niche is US-only.

## Open Questions

- Does the incumbent's launch failure reproduce on a current device?
- What does Apple Search Ads cost on the incumbent's brand term?
- Does iOS 27's SwiftData expose shared CloudKit zones for partner sharing?
