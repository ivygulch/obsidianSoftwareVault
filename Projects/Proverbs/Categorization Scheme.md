# Applying Proverbs — Categorization Scheme (draft)

A first draft of the structure that [[Applying Proverbs]] needs before any passage can be categorized. Everything here is a starting point for the categorization pass, not a decision.

## Unit of categorization

Proverbs mixes one-verse sayings (most of chapters 10–29), multi-verse sayings and numerical sayings (chapters 30–31, parts of 22–24), and extended discourses (chapters 1–9). The unit should be the **passage**: one verse where the saying is one verse, a verse range where it is not. A verse can belong to one passage only; a passage can carry any number of categories. This keeps references exact ("Proverbs 26:4–5") and lets flash cards and reminders show a whole thought.

## Category types

Each type is a separate axis. A passage can carry categories from several types at once. Starter list, with the kinds of values each would hold:

| Type | What it answers | Example values |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** | What is happening to or around the person | conflict with a neighbor, being wronged, a decision under pressure, lending money, correcting a child, being flattered, facing a powerful person, prosperity, poverty, grief |
| **Attitude** | The inner stance the passage addresses | anger, pride, humility, envy, fear, contentment, impatience, greed, gratitude |
| **Characteristic** | The trait of a wise or foolish person the passage describes | the seven Wisdom Deck traits (trusts in God, exercises self-control, walks in healthy relationships, keeps balance, seeks good counsel, manages resources, speaks carefully), plus their opposites and others such as diligence, laziness, honesty, discretion, teachability |
| **Result** | The outcome the passage promises or warns of | ruin, honor, poverty, long life, shame, peace, favor, strife, security |
| **Domain** | The area of life | speech, money and work, family, friendship, leadership and rulers, justice, the fear of the Lord, learning and instruction, food and drink, the body |
| **Role** | Who the passage is about or addressed to | son, parent, king, friend, neighbor, servant, wife, the poor, the rich, the scoffer, the sluggard |
| **Form** | How the saying works | antithetical pair, better-than saying, numerical saying, instruction, warning, observation |

Open: whether Domain and Situation are one axis or two; whether Form is worth exposing to users or only to the categorizer; whether Characteristic should keep the deck's seven as a fixed top level.

## Keywords

Free terms per passage drawn from its own words (scales, sluggard, tongue, ant, gate) and from its sense (bribery, gossip). Keywords drive search and automatic links; they are not categories.

## Links

- **Automatic:** passages sharing a category value, a keyword, or a tag.
- **Editorial:** hand-made links where two passages belong together regardless of vocabulary (26:4 and 26:5; the repeated sayings such as 14:12 and 16:25).
- **User:** any passage to any passage, with an optional note.

## User tags

A single flat tag space per user, applied to passages. Tags are the user's; categories are the app's. Both are searchable and both generate links. Open: whether a user can hide or rename app categories.

## Context groups

A user-created group names an ongoing situation, a person, or a project and holds passages, notes, and optional reminder settings. A passage can be in many groups. Groups are the thing the AI tier would read as background when matching a new situation.

## Starter sources for the pass

- The deck's seven traits for the Characteristic axis ([[Wisdom Deck Reference]]).
- Standard topical outlines of Proverbs (study Bible subject indexes, Kidner's and Waltke's commentaries) as a check on coverage; their category names are not reused verbatim where they are a publisher's editorial work.
- The repeated and near-repeated sayings in the book as the first set of editorial links.
