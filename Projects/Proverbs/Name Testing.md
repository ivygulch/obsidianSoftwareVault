# Proverbs — Name Testing

> How to narrow the app name once candidates exist. Candidates and the naming landscape are in [[App Store Titles and Descriptions]]; the product name is an open scaffold decision in [[Projects/Proverbs/Design|Design]]. Platform facts below are from vendor pages and third-party guides fetched 2026-10-26; budget and sample figures are planning estimates from agency blogs, not published platform minimums.

## What an ad A/B test measures, and what it does not

A Meta ad test with the name as the only variable measures which name makes a cold viewer stop and click. Doug has run this on story titles using a flat single-color background with text only, which removes the image as a confound and makes a text-on-color card close to how a name appears in an App Store search results list.

It does not measure findability. Name keywords carry the most weight in App Store search ranking, and the [[App Store Titles and Descriptions|store research]] shows this niche is reached by searching "proverbs". A scripture-phrase name can win the click test and lose the search test to a Proverbs-forward name. So: run the keyword check first, then ad-test only the names that pass it.

Other limits: app-name effects are smaller than story-title effects, so more impressions per variant are needed; Meta's faith-interest audiences skew to daily-verse and devotional users, which the ISA's user is not, so target "Bible study" or "Book of Proverbs" interests rather than broad Christian inspiration.

Where the test earns its cost: the split between Proverbs-forward names and scripture-phrase names (Fitly Spoken, In Season, Apples of Gold). If a phrase name holds its own on a flat card with no explanation, it is not too opaque. A fixed one-line descriptor under every name ("Find the proverb for right now") keeps the comparison fair and mimics the subtitle slot.

## Candidate names (2026-10-26)

Proverbs-forward: Proverbs Map, Proverbs Trails, Proverbs Threads, Proverbs Woven, Proverbs (bare; "Proverb" exists, "Proverbs++" holds the URL slug, confirm in App Store Connect).
From the book: Fitly Spoken (25:11), In Season (15:23), Apples of Gold (25:11), Straight Paths (3:6), Get Wisdom (4:7).
Hybrid: Proverbs in Season (keyword first, 15:23 phrase; no collision found 2026-10-26; "In Season" alone is owned by produce-calendar apps; the risk to test is whether cold viewers read "for the moment" or "time of year").
Concept: Meander, Mishlei (Hebrew name of the book), Wisdom Cloud.
Doug's additions, checked 2026-10-26: Proverbs Now (no collision; keyword first; "Now" is a generic suffix with no meaning of its own, the same promise as Proverbs in Season with less behind it). Wisdom for Today (in use as the subtitle of "Pax: Catholic Wisdom for Today"; neighborhood is daily-wisdom and stoic quote apps; "Today" is the daily-devotional frame; drops the keyword). Wisdom Path (no exact collision; neighborhood is KarmaPath, Daily Stoic Path, GitaPath, oracle and astrology apps; "path" fits the trail idea, the neighborhood does not; drops the keyword). Wise Counsel (no collision; neighborhood is psychic advice, AI advice chat, couples coaching; "counsel" promises advice, which the ISA forbids the app to give; drops the keyword).
Situational Proverbs (no collision; keyword present; the most literal statement of the ISA's use case; "situational" is HR and safety vocabulary, so the store's traffic for it comes from situational-leadership and situational-awareness apps, and the name reads as a reference tool rather than a devotional, which matches the positioning). Proverbs for Problems (no collision; keyword first; alliterative and plain; "problems" narrows the use to trouble, while the ISA's situations include a person, a project, or prosperity, and it edges toward the advice expectation). Both are descriptive rather than evocative: they would test well on comprehension and weakly on memorability, and their wording may serve better as the subtitle under an evocative name.
Proverbs for Decisions (no collision; keyword first; "decision under pressure" is already a Situation value in the Categorization Scheme and Proverbs Daily's copy uses "one real decision today"; narrower than situations, less negative than problems; lightly implies the app helps decide). Proverbs Applied and Applied Proverbs (no collision; keyword present; "applied" is corporate and academic vocabulary in the store, Applied Systems, Applied Nutrition, Applied AI Academy; the idea note's own title was "Applying Proverbs", where the gerund puts the applying on the user, while "applied" can read as already done by the app, which the ISA's no-interpretation constraint argues against; "Applied Proverbs" reads like a course title, "Proverbs Applied" like a book title).
Applying Proverbs (no collision; keyword present; the idea note's original title; the gerund puts the applying on the user, which is the ISA's posture; as a name it reads as an activity or a study series rather than a thing, and "applying" has no store footing of its own). Practical Wisdom (no exact collision; neighborhood is Ritual: Spiritual Practice, Mindful Dawn, Om: Vedic Wisdom, Stoic Widgets, Taoist Wisdom, Daily Dharma; the phrase is Aristotle's phronesis in common use and reads as generic self-help or Eastern practice; drops the keyword; Solomon's Wisdom, Daily Wisdom, and Wisdom are already taken by Proverbs apps, so a fourth Wisdom name would sit among them without the keyword that distinguishes it).
Creator's Wisdom (no collision; drops the keyword; the first result for the phrase is "Quote It - Creator Assist", and in the store "creator" now means content creator before it means God; the book's own word is "Maker" (14:31, 17:5, 22:2); the intent, that the wisdom is God's rather than Solomon's, is a real distinction from the taken "Solomon's Wisdom", but a cold reader will not get it from the name; same Wisdom-neighborhood problem as the other Wisdom names).
Subtitle candidates: "Proverbs by situation", "Proverbs, linked like a wiki", "Follow the threads, keep the trail", "Find the proverb for right now".

## Platforms, by the question each answers

| Platform | Question it answers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Meta Ads Experiments | Which name makes a cold viewer stop and click | 2 to 5 variations, one variable, audience split without overlap, estimated statistical power shown at setup. Third-party planning figures: about 500 link clicks per variation over 3 to 7 days, $15 to $25 per variation per day; Meta publishes no minimum. |
| Google Ads search | Which name a person already searching clicks | Bid on "proverbs app" and "book of proverbs app"; one ad group per candidate with the headline pinned; compare click-through on the same queries. Closest available proxy to App Store search behavior. |
| PickFu | Why people choose a name; whether a phrase name is understood | Paid respondent panel, 50 per poll, religious-affiliation targeting includes Christianity, up to four trait categories combined with AND logic, every respondent writes a reason. Stated preference, not behavior; use beside an ad test. |
| Apple Search Ads | Not usable for names | Custom product pages vary icon, screenshots, and preview; the name cannot vary. |
| Reddit Ads | Weak fit | Can reach Bible-study communities; volumes small, split tooling crude. |

## Suggested order

1. Keyword check against the scores in [[App Store Titles and Descriptions]]; drop names with no search footing unless the subtitle will carry "Proverbs".
2. Name availability in App Store Connect for the survivors.
3. PickFu poll of the finalists with the descriptor line, for comprehension and reasons.
4. Meta or Google split on the top two or three, flat background, descriptor line fixed.
