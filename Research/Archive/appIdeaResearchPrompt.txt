I want to find new app ideas that a solo indie iOS developer could build well and that have a market large enough to
  matter. Run this as a research project and file the results in this vault.

  KNOBS
  - Candidates to surface in the wide scan: 50
  - Candidates to screen: 12
  - Deep dives: 5
  - Platforms: iOS first; note Android demand where it changes the picture
  - Excluded up front: anything whose core value is dictation or transcription; card games and licensed-IP game
  adaptations (already researched)

  WHAT IS DIFFERENT THIS TIME
  Earlier idea research in this vault weighted ideas toward my own background. Do not do that here. Assume I can learn
  any domain well enough to build for it. Do not use my experience, interests, or the personal-background memory as a
  filter or a tiebreaker. The only two tests are: is there a viable market for an indie, and can one developer build and
  maintain it at a quality that competes.

  What "viable for an indie" means: my income is a portfolio of consulting, apps, and writing, so no single app has to
  replace a salary. A small app with modest recurring revenue and low upkeep is a win. Judge each idea as a portfolio
  member, and do not argue for or against an idea on schedule or timing grounds.

  What "solo-buildable" means: one person can ship a version that is clearly better than the incumbents on the things
  users complain about, and keep it running without a team. Treat these as strikes, and say which apply: a content
  treadmill, a two-sided marketplace or network effect, heavy backend operations or support load, dependence on a
  license or a partner's permission, hardware, regulated liability such as medical-device claims, and a core feature
  Apple or a general AI assistant is likely to absorb.

  BEFORE STARTING
  Read the vault's existing research so you build on it and do not repeat it: every landscape note under
  Research/Competitors, the App Ideas MOC and its area folders, and App Ideas/Shelved with the reasons each idea was
  shelved. List in one short paragraph what is already covered and exclude those niches unless you have new evidence.

  HOW TO RUN IT
  A previous session died waiting on one very large response, so keep the main thread light. Fan the work out to
  parallel subagents, one per source or per candidate. Each agent writes its full findings to a file in the scratchpad
  directory and replies with a summary under 300 words. Keep individual tool outputs small: fetch reviews 50 at a time,
  search results 5 to 10 at a time. Synthesize in the main thread from the files.

  Phase 1, wide scan. Generate candidates from evidence, not brainstorming. Each candidate is one line plus the signal
  that surfaced it. Sources, one agent each:
  - App Store charts by category, paid and top grossing, especially the smaller categories (Reference, Utilities,
  Productivity, Navigation, Weather, Business, Education, Sports, Food & Drink, Lifestyle, Music, Photo & Video,
  Medical). The legacy iTunes RSS feeds with a genre id return these as JSON through curl.
  - Stale incumbents: apps with large rating counts and no update in two or more years, or whose 100 most recent reviews
  are majority one-star. The iTunes Search API returns rating count, release date, and last update date.
  - Indie revenue disclosures: Indie Hackers, RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps, r/iOSProgramming and
  r/SideProject, build-in-public posts, the Sub Club podcast and similar talks on YouTube (use the transcript tool).
  - Unmet-demand language: Reddit and forum threads of the form "is there an app that", "I would pay for", "why is there
  no". Use web search restricted to those sites; Reddit pages often block direct fetch. The Hacker News Algolia API
  works through plain fetch.
  - New platform surface: frameworks and capabilities added in iOS 26 and iOS 27 that few shipped apps use yet. The
  vault has notes on both under Research/Tech. For API facts, the cupertino MCP is the first choice; if it is not
  connected, Apple's documentation JSON endpoint works through curl, while fetching the HTML documentation pages returns
  only a title.
  - Spending niches: hobbies, trades, and professions where people already pay for tools or equipment and the existing
  apps are weak.

  Phase 2, screen. Cut to the screening count using quick store checks only. For each survivor record: the top three
  existing apps with publisher, model, price, rating, US rating count, and last update; whether paid or subscription
  apps in the niche have meaningful rating counts; and which solo-buildability strikes apply. State why each rejected
  candidate was cut in one line.

  Phase 3, deep dive. One agent per finalist. Each produces:
  - Market table for the top five to eight apps, US storefront, plus Germany, France, the UK, and Japan where the niche
  is regional.
  - Review mining with the appstore MCP: star distribution of the 100 most recent reviews beside the lifetime score, top
  praise and complaint themes, verbatim quotes with star and date, and any place a reviewer names a price they would
  pay.
  - Search demand: keyword popularity and difficulty from the astro MCP if it is connected, otherwise the appstore MCP's
  keyword tools. Say which was used.
  - Monetization evidence: what the paying apps charge, how users react to that model, and a revenue range for a
  competent entrant with the method shown and marked as an estimate.
  - Build scope: the smallest version that beats incumbents on the top complaints, the frameworks it needs, and the
  ongoing upkeep.
  - Risks, including platform or AI absorption and any dependency on a third party.

  Phase 4, synthesis. Rank the finalists on a scorecard with one row per idea and these columns: evidence of paying
  demand, incumbent weakness, search demand against difficulty, solo build fit, upkeep, absorption risk, and reach (a
  channel one person can actually use). Give each cell a short evidence phrase, not just a score.

  RULES OF EVIDENCE
  Mark every figure that was not directly fetched as an estimate or unverified. Do not turn a tool's opaque score into a
  real-world claim. Rating count is a size proxy, not revenue. Lifetime ratings are inflated by rating prompts; the
  recent review stream is the product's actual state. An empty niche can mean no demand, so look for demand evidence
  outside the store before calling it an opening. Report negatives and gaps as plainly as findings.

  OUTPUT
  - One synthesis note at Research/Trends/Indie App Opportunity Scan — [date].md with the scorecard, the ranked
  finalists, the rejected candidates with reasons, and the gaps.
  - One landscape note per deep dive under Research/Competitors, following the existing landscape notes' format, linked
  from the Competitors MOC.
  - One seed note per finalist under App Ideas, in the fitting area folder, using the App Idea template with status:
  seed, linked from that area's MOC.
  - Use the Research Note template's confidence field. Do not hard-wrap prose.
  - Do not commit. I will review first.

  Finish with a short message: the ranked finalists in one line each, what you could not verify, and where the files
  are.
