# Proverbs & Situational Scripture Apps — Landscape

> Competitor scan for [[Applying Proverbs]]. Store figures fetched 2026-10-03 (US storefront; Android where noted). Features marked (per listing) were read from store descriptions, not tested.

Scope: US App Store (primary) plus Google Play where it changes the picture, surveyed 2026-10-03. Method: iTunes Search API sweep of 31 terms (487 rows, 229 unique apps), iTunes Lookup for full listings, App Store web pages for in-app purchase prices, App Store RSS/MCP review feeds (50 most recent per app), Play Store MCP search/details, and web search for The Wisdom Deck and public Proverbs topic indexes. Ratings and counts are US-store figures on the survey date. Items marked (per listing) come from store descriptions only; items marked (unverified) could not be confirmed.

## Summary

What exists: a thin tier of Proverbs-specific iOS apps, all built around either "chapter of the day" (Proverbs has 31 chapters) or "one curated verse a day," none with more than 1,101 US ratings, most solo-developer, several abandoned. Topical lookup exists in two forms: 19th-century topical concordances repackaged (Nave's, Torrey's), and small "verses by topic" lists of 12–220 categories (bigger on Android than iOS). "Find a verse for my situation" is now dominated by AI Bible-chat apps (Bible Chat 364k ratings, Haven 149k, Creed 68k) selling weekly subscriptions, and by YouVersion's "How are you feeling?" emoji search. The single closest feature-set analog is Bible Topics and Quiz (Daniel Olaogun): topics, connected tags between topics, per-verse journals, daily verse, reminders, spaced-repetition quiz, offline — but across the whole Bible, with 1 US rating.

What does not exist: any app that categorizes all of Proverbs by multiple orthogonal schemes (situations, attitudes, characteristics, results); user-defined tags on Proverbs passages; wiki-style links between passages; a "context group" that collects passages for an ongoing situation; App Intents/Shortcuts quick capture; or a Proverbs-only AI situation matcher. No shipped app combines curated categorization with AI matching; the AI apps have no curated index and the curated apps have no AI.

## Review mining

Star split is of the most recent reviews available (50 unless fewer exist).

Proverb-A-Day (39 reviews total, 2013–2026): 25×5, 3×4, 3×3, 4×2, 4×1. Praise: the daily habit ("It only takes a second to read it, and believe me kinda sets a mental tone for the day") and the commentary ("I appreciate how the commentaries are aimed at sharing biblical truth without watering it down"). Complaints: breakage ("After last Apple update 26-whatever it just gives an ERROR MESSAGE!!"), ad quality ("Sometimes the banner ad at the bottom shows porn"), paid features not working ("Paid version same as free, 'Show Commentary' does not show"), text accuracy ("Your proverb 25:20 was written wrong"), and two separate requests for a widget. Price statements: both IAPs are $0.99; one reviewer bought both and still got no commentary.

Proverbs & Psalms – King James (8 reviews total): 4×5, 1×4, 2×3, 1×1. Praise is generic ("very uplifting"). Complaints: ads ("Too many ads"; "annoying the way the ads take over"), rate-me prompts mid-reading ("4 times in reading my daily scripture (it was only 8 verses) I was prompted to rate this app"), audio state bugs. No price statements.

Bible Widget (50): 48×5, 1×4, 1×2. Praise centers on topic-driven widget verses meeting a mood ("i love when i feel a way and i'm able to just look at my widget and see a bible verse that helps my feelings"; "I love that I can pick the version because KJV is my #1 choice"). Complaint: "$30 a year?? Kind of ridiculous, no thanks!!!" One request for Greek NT/Septuagint.

#Bible – Verse of the Day (50): 45×5, 2×4, 1×3, 2×2. Praise: widgets and perceived relevance ("lately, they have been spot on with what I need to hear/read"; "It even has a search bar to search whatever you want"). Complaints are paywall-shaped: "it makes you go through a very long process just to tell you the first seven days are free, then you have to pay. PAY FOR A BIBLE APP?"; "have to pay for too many things that used to be free before."

Bible Chat (50): 47×5, 1×4, 2×1. Praise: personalization ("This app customizes your daily time with God towards what is going on with YOU!"; "Fixed my Life!"). Complaints: subscription handling ("Didnt ask me if I like the app to continue the subscription") and a forced survey in onboarding. Price statement: a convert says they had been "paying $20 per month" for a competing app.

Haven (50, added because its split is the outlier): 23×5, 2×4, 5×3, 3×2, 17×1. Seventeen of the one-stars are about price, almost all citing the weekly rate: "$6.99 per week, that's $210.00 a month"; "Paying to read the word of God is ridiculous to me. $7 a week is wild"; "You should never pay for god." Praise: "works exactly the way it is promised." One reviewer reports being charged after unsubscribing.

General Bible apps (150 recent reviews each scanned for topic/situation terms): YouVersion reviews mention keyword search positively and one notes search is online-only. Bible Gateway's recent reviews are dominated by search regressions ("Bring back the search filters!!!") and ads. Logos reviews object to AI search displacing conventional search ("the search engine has been replaced by a pay to play type feature"). Olive Tree reviews praise search and cross-device notes. No scanned review in any of the four asks for situational or topical lookup by name; the asks are for search that works offline, with filters, without ads.

## Public categorization schemes

- The Wisdom Deck (Mark Matlock, Planet Wisdom / WisdomWorks, c. 2010): a card deck with a Proverb on one face and seven "marks of a wise person" on the back, with the applicable marks circled: Trusts in God, Walks in Healthy Relationships, Seeks Good Counsel, Speaks Carefully, Exercises Self-Control, Keeps Balance, Manages Resources. Sold through Real World Parents / Youth Specialties. Card count not found; thewisdomdeck.com no longer resolves; current availability unverified (likely out of print).
- Precept Austin, "Proverbs Topically Arranged" (Bruce Hurt): the full book regrouped under topic headings, plus a separate alphabetical "Proverbs Topical Index"; structural split (1–9 youth, 10–24 Solomon, 25–29 Hezekiah, 30 Agur, 31 Lemuel) is the same one Watchdis uses in its listing.
- A Bible Commentary, "Topical Index of the Proverbs" (PDF): alphabetical keyword index with "See" cross-references (e.g. "ABHOR (See 'Hate')").
- Bible-Discernments, "A Topical Treasury of Proverbs": 100 topics, 336 pages, every verse grouped under subject (Marriage, Wisdom, Finances, etc.).
- BibleStudy.org "Book of Proverbs by Topic"; NEC Church "Proverbs by Category" PDF (2024); Alan Lowne "Proverbs by Topic" PDF; Seiyaku alphabetical keyword index; proverbscollections.com (JavaScript-rendered, content not readable by fetch; unverified).
- Whole-Bible indexes: Nave's Topical Bible (6,000+ headings / 20,000 topics and sub-topics, 100,000 refs; public domain), Torrey's Topical Textbook (628 entries, 20,000 refs; public domain), OpenBible.info topics (crowd-voted verses per phrase).
- Every scheme found is single-axis (topic). None indexes by situation, attitude, characteristic and result as separate dimensions, and none records result/consequence pairings (e.g. "diligence → wealth") as a first-class category.

## Observations

1. The largest Proverbs-only iOS app has 1,101 US ratings and is ad-supported; the next has 514. Everything else is under 15.
2. Five new Proverbs-only iOS apps shipped between 2025-01 and 2026-06 (Solomon's Wisdom, Proverbs++, Daily: Psalms & Proverbs, Wisdom: Daily Proverbs Verse, Proverbs Daily), all solo or near-solo, all built on chapter-of-the-day or verse-of-the-day.
3. No Proverbs app on either store categorizes the book by topic; Proverbs & Psalms KJV presents it as plain chapters. Topic organization of Proverbs exists only on the web and in print.
4. Topical indexes on iOS are either public-domain concordances (Nave's, Torrey's) in ad-supported shells or solo-built lists of 68–220 categories with under 60 ratings each. Android has topical-verse apps with thousands of ratings.
5. The "verse for my situation" job at scale is served by AI chat (Bible Chat, Haven, Creed: 581k combined US ratings, all launched 2023–2025) priced at $2.99–$6.99 per week, and by YouVersion's free emoji picker.
6. Weekly pricing is the dominant complaint in AI Bible-chat reviews (Haven: 17 of 50 recent reviews are one-star, nearly all about $6.99/week); "pay for a Bible app" objections also appear in #Bible and Bible Gateway reviews.
7. User-defined tags on verses exist only in memory apps (Fighter Verses: topics, tags, categories on user-added verses). No reading or topical app offers them.
8. Bible Topics and Quiz is the only app found with topic-to-topic links, per-verse journals, spaced repetition and daily reminders in one package; it has 1 US rating and a $0.99 Pro unlock, indicating the feature set alone has not produced discovery.
9. Widgets and lock-screen verses are requested in Proverb-A-Day reviews and are the stated reason for 5-star reviews of Bible Widget and #Bible.
10. Bible versions in the Proverbs tier are public-domain or freely licensable (KJV, WEB, ASV, NET); the solo apps that list NIV/ESV do so in a premium tier (Daily Wisdom) or with narration under a company name (Solomon's Wisdom); licensing terms not verified.

## Gaps

- Multi-axis categorization of Proverbs (situation, attitude, characteristic, result) — not found anywhere, app or web.
- User tags and user notes on Proverbs passages in a Proverbs-focused app — not found; nearest is Fighter Verses (tags on memory verses) and Bible Topics and Quiz (journals on any verse).
- Wiki-style passage-to-passage links — not found; nearest is Bible Topics and Quiz (topic-to-topic via tags) and Logos/YouVersion cross-references.
- Context groups for an ongoing situation — not found in any app surveyed.
- Quick capture via App Intents/Shortcuts/Siri — not mentioned in any listing surveyed.
- Flash cards drawn from Proverbs with categorization on the back (the Wisdom Deck pattern) — not found in app form; the Wisdom Deck itself appears unavailable.
- A Proverbs-scoped AI matcher (situation in, passages out, constrained to a curated index) — not found; the AI apps answer from the whole Bible and sell the chat, not the index.
- Offline, ad-free, non-subscription Proverbs reference with search — partially met by Daily Wisdom (Bergeron) and Solomon's Wisdom; neither has categories.
